How We Tested

To make this comparison fair and reproducible, we used three documents of different lengths and complexities:

Document Type Length Complexity
Research paper PDF 12 pages / 4,800 words High
News article URL 2,000 words Medium
Product review Pasted text 800 words Low

We scored each tool on three criteria (1-5 scale):

  • Accuracy — Did the summary capture the main points? Any hallucinations or omissions?
  • Speed — How long from upload to summary?
  • Free tier value — How generous is the free plan? What limits exist?

The Contenders

2. ChatGPT (GPT-4o)

Accuracy: 4/5 Speed: 4/5 Free tier: 3/5

ChatGPT is the most well-known AI tool, but it's a general-purpose chatbot — not a dedicated summarizer. You paste text or upload a file and ask it to summarize.

Research paper: Good summary quality, but required careful prompting ("Summarize this paper, including research question, methodology..." ). Without specific instructions, it sometimes wrote overly general summaries.

News article: Delivered a clean summary in seconds, but occasionally included minor factual drift — the summary said "3 main reasons" when the article had 4.

Product review: Handled well. Simple text summarization is ChatGPT's strength.

Free tier: GPT-4o has message limits on the free plan (roughly 15-20 messages every 3 hours). No file upload on free tier — you must copy-paste text. PDF upload requires ChatGPT Plus ($20/month).

Best for: Quick text-only summaries when you're already using ChatGPT for other tasks. Not ideal for PDF-heavy workflows.

3. QuillBot Summarizer

Accuracy: 3.5/5 Speed: 4.5/5 Free tier: 2/5

QuillBot is primarily a paraphrasing tool, but its summarizer mode is popular. It offers two modes: Key Sentences (extractive) and Paragraph (abstractive).

Research paper: The Key Sentences mode worked surprisingly well — it pulled the most important sentences verbatim. But the Paragraph mode produced a choppy, unnatural summary that read like disconnected facts strung together.

News article: Decent results in Paragraph mode. Key Sentences mode simply returned 4 sentences from the article, which is less of a summary and more of a highlight reel.

Product review: Fine for short text. Key Sentences mode was actually useful here — it identified the verdict sentences efficiently.

Free tier: Only 1,200 words per summary on the free plan. Premium ($9.95/month) raises it to 6,000 words. No PDF upload — text input only.

Best for: Short articles and quick extractions when you just need the key sentences.

4. SMMRY

Accuracy: 2.5/5 Speed: 5/5 Free tier: 5/5

SMMRY is a minimalist extractive summarizer — it scores sentences by keyword frequency and returns the highest-scoring ones. No AI generation, no understanding of meaning.

Research paper: Returned sentences from the abstract, introduction, and conclusion — roughly what a human would skim first. Missed the actual findings entirely because they contained less keyword repetition.

News article: Worked reasonably well for a straightforward news structure (inverted pyramid). The summary felt coherent because news articles front-load important information.

Product review: Poor. Returned sentences about shipping and packaging — not the product quality — because those words appeared frequently.

Free tier: Completely free. No sign-up. No limits. But also no AI — you get what you pay for.

Best for: Quick, rough summaries of news articles. Not suitable for academic or technical content.

5. TLDR This

Accuracy: 3/5 Speed: 4/5 Free tier: 3/5

TLDR This is a Chrome extension and web app that uses a mix of extractive and abstractive summarization. It markets itself as a tool for "information overload."

Research paper: Decent extractive summary — captured the abstract-level information well. But like SMMRY, it missed nuance in the methodology and discussion sections.

News article: Strong performance. The AI-generated summary was readable and accurate. This is clearly TLDR This's sweet spot — web articles.

Product review: Good summary that correctly identified the product's main selling points and criticisms. The bullet-point mode was helpful here.

Free tier: 10 free summaries with sign-up. Premium ($4/month) adds unlimited summaries, AI summaries, and keyword extraction. No PDF upload on free tier.

Best for: Web articles and blog posts. The Chrome extension is convenient for browsing.

What to Look for in an Online AI Summarizer

Based on our testing, here are the features that separate the best tools from the rest:

  1. Multi-format input: The best summarizer handles PDFs, URLs, and pasted text equally well. PDF support is especially critical for academic and professional use — most free tools lack it entirely.
  2. Structured output: A wall of text isn't useful. Look for tools that separate the summary into sections: main argument, key findings, methodology, limitations. Structured output saves you the work of reorganizing.
  3. Multiple summary modes: Sometimes you want a 3-sentence TLDR. Other times you need bulleted key points. A good summarizer offers both — plus translation if you work across languages.
  4. Generous free tier: Many tools advertise "free" but impose tiny word limits or force sign-up. The best free tiers let you test thoroughly before committing. 10 free summaries per day should be the minimum bar.
  5. No hallucinations: This is the non-negotiable. An AI that invents facts or misattributes claims is worse than useless. Test any tool with content you know well before trusting it with unfamiliar material.
